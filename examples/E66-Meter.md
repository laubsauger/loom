# E66 — Meter

The audio analysis, shown: one `AudioAnalysis` component on a bound clip, and every lane it publishes owns one visible thing. The core breathes with the bass, jumps on the kick and glows a halo whose spread is the loudness, whose brightness is the hat band and whose hue is the spectral centroid; the ring flashes on the snare; sparks scatter on every hat; the backdrop blinks on any onset; and a hand sweeps once round per bar, brightest on the beat, from the source's declared tempo. The clip is synthesised, so what plays in which bar is written down — and when the hats drop out for four bars, then everything stops for two, you watch each lane lose exactly the thing it owns while the hand keeps turning.

## Why a file, and why this file

Every audio example before this one drives from the deterministic test pattern, with a file behind a switch nobody flips. That demonstrates the graph and never the analysis: a pattern's "kick" is a band envelope the pattern wrote itself. This one binds a clip — `media/showcase-beat.m4a`, generated by `src/examples/build-showcase-beat.ts` — under the timeline lock, so a scrub is bit-exact (T1229), and it declares the tempo (T1228) so `beatPhase` and `barPhase` are facts about the file rather than estimates.

The clip is 124 BPM, 4/4, one silent lead-in beat then 16 bars: 48 kHz mono, 31.45 s. Beat one is at 0.484 s, which is the source's Beat Offset.

| bars | what plays |
| --- | --- |
| 1–4 | full: kick on every beat, snare on 2 and 4, closed hats on every eighth, a held bass note, a ping on odd downbeats |
| 5–8 | hats dropped |
| 9–10 | silence |
| 11–16 | full again, with an open hat on the last off-beat of every bar |

At the detector's defaults the analysis counts exactly **56 kicks**, **28 snares** and **75 hats** across the clip (bars 1–4: 32 hats; bar 11: 8; bars 12–16: 7 each, because the open hat rings through the next downbeat, which therefore carries no closed one). `src/examples/e66-showcase.test.ts` measures those three numbers off the generator and reads this table against the generator's own statement of the grid, so the two cannot drift.

## The graph

```
clip1(audioFileIn) -> analysis1 -+-> lvl1(valueSelect)   the conditioned lanes, ranked over 16 s
                                 +-> hit1(valueSelect)   the counts, decaying over 250 ms

bg1(solid) -> flash1(level) ----------------------------------------------+
core1(circle) -> kick1(transform) -> halo1(blur) -> glow1(level) -> tint1(hsv) -+
ringo1(circle) -> ring1(multiply) <- hole1(circle) ; ring1 -> snap1(level) -----+-> mix1(add) -> output1(output)
grain1(noise) -> spark1(threshold) -> hats1(level) --------------------------+
hand1(rectangle) -> sweep1(transform) -> tick1(level) -----------------------+
kick1 ---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

`analysis1` is the starter component `AudioAnalysis`, unchanged. Its `levels` output is the source's bands and centroid after the envelope and the running-rank normaliser; its `hits` output is the four counts, each held at 1 on the frame it fires and decaying over `hitDecay` milliseconds. The two Selects pass each bag through unchanged at their default `*` — they exist so each bag has a name an expression can reach. They were Limits at 0…1 until T1302b, which is the identity only on lanes that live there: the hits bag also carries the tempo claim, and the Limit clipped `bpm` 124 to 1 and `beat` and `bar` to at most 1.

## The legend, lane by lane

Every lane owns exactly one visual, so cutting a lane removes one thing and nothing else. That is what `e66-meter-claims.gpu.test.ts` asserts, one claim per row.

| lane | drives | what you see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `levels.low` | `core1` radius | the core breathes with the held bass note; it re-centres after the silence as the normaliser's window refills |
| `levels.level` | `halo1` blur size | the halo spreads with how loud the whole mix is |
| `levels.high` | `glow1` brightness | the halo's brightness is the hat band, which is why it dims for bars 5–8 and is back for 11–16 |
| `levels.centroid` | `tint1` hue offset | the halo is warm when the spectrum sits low (hats dropped) and swings cool when the hats and the ping pull it up |
| `hits.kickCount` | `kick1` scale | the core jumps a third larger on every beat and falls back over 250 ms |
| `hits.snareCount` | `snap1` brightness | the ring flashes on 2 and 4 |
| `hits.hatCount` | `hats1` brightness | sparks scatter on every eighth — none in bars 5–8, and in bars 12–16 the downbeat has none because the open hat before it is still ringing |
| `hits.onsetCount` | `flash1` brightness | the backdrop blinks on every onset, drums of any kind |
| `clip1.barPhase` | `sweep1` rotation | the hand sweeps once round per bar |
| `clip1.beatPhase` | `tick1` brightness | the hand is brightest on the beat and fades towards the next |

The two phase lanes are read from the **source**, not the component. §V952 draws the line: a conditioned level goes through `levels`, a count goes through `hits`, and a phase is neither — it is not a level to rank and not a count to decay, so the component does not touch it.

Bars 9–10 are the demonstration of that line. Every lane the analysis drives goes to rest: the core shrinks to its floor, the halo closes, the ring and the sparks are gone, the backdrop is still. The hand keeps turning at the same rate, because a declared tempo is a fact about the file and not a thing that has to be heard. That is the difference between a lane that listens and a lane that counts.

## What a host without the clip sees

Every driven parameter carries a retained value, and together they are the picture at rest: a mid-size core, a soft halo, no ring, no sparks, the hand at twelve. That is what a headless render with nothing on the audio seam would show — and it is not what the example gates render. `src/examples/shipped-clip-audio.ts` recognises the bound file, renders the generator's samples, walks them through the app's own offline analysis at the gate's frame rate, and feeds the result to the harness the way the app feeds a pre-analysed track under the timeline lock. The liveness baselines, the card and every claim above are the picture with the sound in it.
